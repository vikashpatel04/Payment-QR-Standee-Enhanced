E-ink customer displays, in beta: a Raspberry Pi standee on the shop Wi-Fi, or an ESP32 board on USB. Installed copies update to it automatically.

## New

- **Wi-Fi E-ink display (beta).** A Raspberry Pi with a Waveshare e-Paper HAT becomes a standee that shows the exact amount's QR. One install command on the Pi ([clients/raspberry-pi](../../tree/main/clients/raspberry-pi)), then **Displays → Add display → Wi-Fi E-ink display**: the app finds it on the network (or type its name, such as `raspberrypi.local`), and you pair it with the 6-digit code on its screen.
- **Only your PC can drive it.** After pairing, the display shows nothing from anyone else on the shop Wi-Fi, and its key never crosses the network again. **Remove** makes it forget your PC.
- **USB E-ink display (beta).** A Waveshare e-Paper panel on an ESP32 board, plugged in by one USB cable. Flash the firmware once, then **Displays → Add display → USB E-ink display**. The app finds the board by itself and never sends anything to other COM devices, such as receipt printers. A Raspberry Pi can use USB too. Wiring and flashing: [docs/HARDWARE.md](../../blob/main/docs/HARDWARE.md).
- **A standee layout for small upright panels:** the QR almost edge to edge, your shop name in bold, then the amount.
- **"Your UPI QR" slide** in **Content**: your standee QR with no amount, under your shop name, with your own message such as "Happy Shopping!". Customers type the amount, like the printed standee.
- **The display keeps the QR safe on its own.** It clears a QR when its time runs out, or 10 seconds after the PC goes quiet.
- **Check wiring** and **Pair again**: if a display is reachable but isn't drawing, or no longer knows your PC, it says so and counts as offline, so the cashier is warned that the customer can't see the QR.
- **Build your own display:** [docs/DISPLAY-PROTOCOL.md](../../blob/main/docs/DISPLAY-PROTOCOL.md) describes what any board has to do.

**Why beta:** the Raspberry Pi display is tested end to end on a Pi Zero 2 W with a 2.13" HAT (about 4.5 seconds per screen). The ESP32 firmware is tested with a real board, but no ESP32-driven panel has been confirmed drawing yet. If you try other hardware, please open an issue saying whether it worked.
